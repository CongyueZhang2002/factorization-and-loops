# Two-body angular integrals and threshold distributions

The provider reduces a typed massless two-particle unit-cut family at fixed
observed momentum. It preserves the declared Lorentz-invariant measure and
routing Jacobian. Angular denominators are A_i(1-beta_i.n), where the scale and
spatial Gram matrix are derived from the original affine on-shell inverse
propagators. Integer raised powers and polynomial numerators reduce by
tangential integration by parts to the normalized volume, one massive single
seed and one pair seed. Two massive positive-power directions require prior
partial fractions or a further provider and currently fail explicitly.

`ReduceTwoBodyAngularPowers`, `ConstructTwoBodyAngularGeometry` and
`EvaluateTwoBodyCutIntegral` implement the exact reduction. The explicit seeds
are the standard Gauss and one-mass Appell functions; see
[Somogyi, arXiv:1101.3557](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.3557).
`ExpandTwoBodyAngularSeeds` supplies finite logarithm/dilogarithm coefficients,
with an epsilon-tail audit and a failure for unsupported higher orders.

## Endpoint continuation

A fixed-interior epsilon expansion is insufficient to determine endpoint delta
coefficients. For a coalescing null pair, v(t)->0, the exact connection is

    2F1(1,1;1-e;1-v)
      = Gamma(1-e) Gamma(1+e) v^(-1-e) (1-v)^e
        + e/(1+e)/(1-v) 2F1(1,1+e;2+e;v/(v-1)).

`ConstructAngularEndpointExpansion` applies this connection before epsilon
expansion, keeps both branches and derives each affine endpoint power. It
constructs the full finite Taylor jet required by that power, through one
additional epsilon order for endpoint moments. `ExtractEndpointDistributions`
then returns explicit delta derivatives, plus distributions and regular terms.
Massive Gauss and Appell arguments must remain in the declared analytic domain.
Mixed regulator/endpoint denominators are rejected pending joint resolution.
Higher derivative/generalized-plus terms may appear in separate integrals;
only their complete amplitude sum can establish cancellation.

## Ordinary prescriptions: same certificate, uniform endpoint scope

`CertifyOrdinaryPrescriptionRemoval` accepts `AngularEndpointDomain` containing
`Variable`, `CoordinateRules` and `Assumptions`, plus `ScalarCoefficients` in the
outer request. This is a specialization of the existing compact-cut proof.
It is limited to unit two-particle cuts, P^2=h t with h>0, a constant dimensional
measure apart from the derived phase volume, and rational external coefficients.
Every active normalized scale must have a nonzero leading coefficient uniformly
on compact tangential subsets. Positive-power angular velocities obey |beta|<=1.
Negative powers contribute a finite polynomial bound; rational coefficient
divisors and any inverse-Gram growth are included in the endpoint power loss B.

For total positive angular power J, Holder bounds the product by the null
single-direction moment

    H_J(sigma) = 2^(-J) Gamma(1-J-sigma) Gamma(2-2sigma)
                 /[Gamma(2-J-2sigma) Gamma(1-sigma)].

A common open domain is Re e < min(0,1-J,1-B), excluding isolated dimensional
normalization poles. The complex-dimensional angular measure follows from
Gamma(3/2-e)/(pi^(3/2) Gamma(-e)) times
(1-|y|^2)^(-1-e) d^3 y on the unit ball. Compact complex-epsilon sets admit a
positive real-weight bound. With the phase volume t^(-e), the joint density is
absolutely integrable there, including merged angular directions.

Ordinary eta limits obey |(D+i eta)^(-j)|<=|D|^(-j). Dominated convergence in
that domain, followed by unique meromorphic distributional continuation,
excludes additional prescription-dependent endpoint atoms. It does not remove
the delta and plus terms generated by dimensional continuation, and it does not
permit expanding epsilon at fixed eta first. Cut prescriptions remain unchanged.
A bound for angular integrals is asserted, not a common pointwise angular bound
for all moving directions. Repeated cut derivatives need additional estimates.

The causal interval witness for a spacelike shifted propagator checks both
V^2<0 and (V+lambda P)^2<0 for lambda r+V. If r and P-r are future causal, a
causal difference would make one of these endpoints causal, a contradiction.
The check includes nonnegative cut-mass increments.
