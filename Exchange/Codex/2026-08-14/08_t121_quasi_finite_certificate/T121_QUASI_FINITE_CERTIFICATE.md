# T121 quasi-finite dimension shift

## Integral and physical chart

The period is

\[
I_{121}(D)=\operatorname{GLI}\!\left[
  \mathrm{CF385},\{1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1\}
\right],
\qquad D=4-2\epsilon .
\]

Its rationalized four-fold representation uses

\[
0<r<1,\qquad a>0,\quad u>0,\quad v>0.
\]

Write (C=1+r^2).  The four distinct ordinary denominators are

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_1&=(C-2aur)^2+v^2(C+2aur)^2,\\
P_2&=(aC+2ur)^2+v^2(aC-2ur)^2,\\
P_3&=(uC+2ar)^2+v^2(uC-2ar)^2,\\
P_4&=(auC-2r)^2+v^2(auC+2r)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The powers are (P_1P_2^2P_3^2P_4).  After (n) pure dimension
shifts, (D\to D+2n), set (epsilon=0).  Up to a constant independent of
(a,u,v,r), the density is

\[
\mathcal I_n=
\frac{
 a^{1+2n}u^{1+2n}v^{2n}r^{1+2n}
 (1-r^2)^{3+4n}
 (1+a^2)^{4-2n}(1+u^2)^{4-2n}
 (1+v^2)^{5-2n}(1+r^2)^{7-6n}
}{P_1P_2^2P_3^2P_4}.
\]

`VerifyT121QuasiFinite.wls` checks this expression directly against the
stored T121 density for (n=0,1,2).

## Why one shift is insufficient

Keep (r,v) in the interior.  For (a\sim u\sim\lambda\to0),

\[
P_2\sim P_3\sim\lambda^2,
\qquad
\mathcal I_1\,da\,du\sim\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.
\]

For (a\sim u\sim\lambda^{-1}\to\infty), the same logarithm occurs:

\[
\mathcal I_1\,da\,du\sim\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.
\]

Consequently the unchanged scalar integral in (D+2) is not quasi-finite.

## Coordinate boundaries after two shifts

Use the scaling convention

\[
a\sim\lambda^A,qquad
u\sim\lambda^U,qquad
v\sim\lambda^V,qquad \lambda\to0^+,
\]

where a positive exponent denotes a zero and a negative exponent denotes an
infinity.  At fixed (0<r<1), the radial convergence degree of
(\mathcal I_2\,da\,du\,dv) is

\[
\delta_{\rm int}
=2|A+U|+4|A-U|+5|V|.
\]

It is strictly positive for every nontrivial coordinate scaling.  If
(r\sim\lambda^R\to0), (R>0), the degree becomes

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{r=0}={}&|A+U+R|+|A+U-R|\\
&+2|A-U-R|+2|U-A-R|+5|V|>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first two absolute values cannot vanish simultaneously because (R>0).
For (1-r\sim\lambda^R\to0),

\[
\delta_{r=1}=\delta_{\rm int}+12R>0.
\]

Thus every ordinary coordinate boundary is integrable in (D+4).

## Real zero sets of the denominator polynomials

(P_2) and (P_3) have no zero in the interior of the physical chamber.
The other two vanish on

\[
v=0,qquad
au=\frac{1+r^2}{2r}quad(P_1=0),
\]

and

\[
v=0,qquad
au=\frac{2r}{1+r^2}quad(P_4=0).
\]

Let (N) be the first square in (P_1) or (P_4), with
(N\sim\lambda^{n_N}), (v\sim\lambda^{n_v}), and (n_N,n_v>0).
Let (h=a/u\sim\lambda^H).  At an interior point of either zero set,

\[
\delta_{P_i}=n_N+5n_v-2\min(n_N,n_v)+4|H|>0.
\]

At the (r\to0) end of the (P_1) locus,

\[
\delta_{P_1,r=0}=
n_N+5n_v-2\min(n_N,n_v)
+2R+2|H-R|+2|H+R|>0.
\]

At the (r\to0) end of the (P_4) locus,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{P_4,r=0}={}&n_N+5n_v+\min(R,n_N)
-2\min\!\left(n_N,n_v+\min(R,n_N)\right)\\
&+2R+2|H-R|+2|H+R|>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first line is at least (4n_v).  The two loci merge at
(r\to1, au\to1, v\to0).  There the common degree is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{\rm merge}={}&12R+4|H|+n_N+5n_v\\
&-2\min(n_N,n_v)-2\min(2R,n_N,n_v)>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, (delta_{\rm merge}\ge 8R+4n_v+4|H|).

These charts exhaust the real zeros because each (P_i) is displayed as a
sum of two squares.

## Result

For the scalar period with its six ordinary propagator powers unchanged,

\[
\boxed{n_{\min}=2},
\]

so the first pure-dimensional quasi-finite representative is in (D+4), not
(D+2).  A (D+2) representative would require an additional numerator that
removes both (a,u\to0) and (a,u\to\infty) logarithms.  Raising any of the
vanishing (P_2) or (P_3) powers makes those logarithms more singular.

The exact weighted census is generated by `AnalyzeT121QuasiFinite.py`; it
finds precisely the two (D+2) logarithms above and no nonpositive degree in
the (D+4) coordinate or denominator-zero charts.
