Below is the consult reply, reasoned entirely from your summary (I did not need the repository; I flag the two places where a repo-level detail — kinematic-point generation and the letter list — decides between branches).

**Executive summary.** (1) A rational-in-ε solution provably exists given your measurements; what is open is only its minimal degree, and none of the ~30 section policies you tried can measure that — they all pick particular lattice points, while minimality is a column-reduction problem over F_p[ε]. The kernel-basis plan is the right primary tool, and I predict it will return a section near sibling scale. (2) Before running it, two cheap kill-shots: re-probe on fresh *random* kinematic points (the single fixed 37-point set is your most dangerous confound) and split the system by (Z/2)³ characters. (3) On general grounds both triple covers are singular models of K3 surfaces — a double cover of your quartic del Pezzo pair-cover branched in |−2K| — so expect provable non-rationalizability à la Festi–van Straten; this is decidable in days, and the partial pair chart (8 grades → 2) is worth adopting regardless of the verdict. (4) Audit the maximal cuts once for non-algebraic periods before trusting the dlog ansatz at all. (5) Strategy: dlog-with-ε-residues plus family-level factorization as primary, numerical transport in parallel as insurance, chart-hunting only as the few-day classification.

## A. The ε-degree question

**A0 (proven statements first).** Since rank A(ε) = 2,208 with exact solvability at 76 regulator values over two prime widths, Schwartz–Zippel gives, with overwhelming probability (the only caveat being that your ε samples are random, not structured), rank [A|b] = rank A = 2,208 over F_p(ε), hence solvability over the rational function field: a rational-in-ε solution exists. Concretely, fix any 2,208×2,208 nonsingular minor; setting the complementary 52 coordinates to zero, Cramer's rule yields an explicit rational section with denominator of degree ≤ 3·2,208 ≈ 6.6·10³ and numerators bounded similarly plus the cleared b-degrees (≤ +15 or so). So "no rational representative exists" is provably false; the open quantity is the minimal degree, bounded above by ~6.7·10³. Likewise, each right kernel vector of M = [A | −b̃] (b̃ = denominator-cleared) can be built from r×r minors, so all 53 minimal indices are ≤ ~6.6·10³; 36 of them are 0 (your common kernel), so the ≤17 nontrivial ones average ≲400. On that scale, ">64" is 1% of the allowed range — not intrinsically alarming. But note what the measurement does and does not say: your fixed-coordinate-order policies *are* bona fide rational sections (degree somewhere in (64, ~6,600]); the minimal section differs from every one of them by ε-dependent combinations of the 16 non-constant kernel directions, which no pointwise policy can discover. Nothing measured so far proves the *minimal* degree exceeds 64.

**A1 (top recommendation — endorse the converged plan, with a prediction).** Compute the shifted minimal (Popov-form) right kernel basis of M = [A | −b̃] over F_p[ε]; among the 53 basis vectors at least one has nonzero last coordinate (the 52 pure-kernel vectors span the last-coordinate-zero subspace), and the minimal such vector gives the minimal-degree solution x = u/v; then reduce u modulo the 52 homogeneous minimal kernel vectors to minimize coordinate-wise degrees. This computes exactly the invariant your section experiments only bounded from below. My prediction [suggested, not proven]: it comes out at or near sibling scale (≲ 30). Reason: item 2 of your impasse — 1,076 coordinates *exactly constant in ε* — is very hard to reconcile with a genuinely enormous minimal degree; the natural reading is a low-degree true solution contaminated in the remaining 1,184 coordinates by the 16 ε-varying kernel directions under every fixed policy. Algorithms and software: order/σ-bases (Beckermann–Labahn, SIMAX 15 (1994) 804; Giorgi–Jeannerod–Villard, ISSAC 2003), minimal nullspace bases (Zhou–Labahn–Storjohann, ISSAC 2012), implemented in PML (Hyun–Neiger–Schost, ISSAC 2019, arXiv:1905.04356; github.com/vneiger/pml) over your 61-bit primes, with adaptive order doubling. At n ≈ 2.3·10³ and output degree in the hundreds this is hours on one node; even the ~6.6·10³ worst case is ≤ days. Your acceptance criteria (disjoint ε, original-row residual, second prime) are right; strengthen "residual" to a polynomial identity check at more ε-probes than the degree bound, and add a held-out *kinematic* point set (see D1).

**A2 (equal-power low-tech alternative and cross-check).** Fixed-minor high-order lifting: pick one nonsingular 2,208-pivot set once, shift ε → ε₀ + δ with A(ε₀) full-rank, lift the square solve δ-adically to precision 2D + O(1) (Storjohann, J. Symb. Comput. 36 (2003) 613, is the asymptotically clean version; plain Dixon lifting suffices), rational-reconstruct per coordinate, then reduce modulo the homogeneous minimal kernel basis. This reuses your modular solver almost unchanged. For scale: even a brute-force sampling of the fixed-pivot Cramer section at the worst case D ≈ 6.6·10³ costs ~1.3·10⁴ images × 4 s ≈ 15 h per prime, embarrassingly parallel over ε — so the feared worst case is affordable either way. Rank below A1 only because minimality arrives after, not during, the computation.

**A3 (preprocessing that lowers D before computing).** (a) Absorb the known ε-rational structure analytically: the (7,6), (8,7), (5,4) classes of b almost certainly factor into a small set of (aε+b) factors of Γ-function/IBP origin; multiply unknowns by the appropriate LCM/Q^m per column (and per grade — see D4) so b becomes polynomial and the normalization matches siblings. This is your "factor Q out of the ansatz" option: it cannot hurt and collapses D if the inflation is a normalization artifact; it cannot by itself explain thousands (a wrong-but-rational normalization shifts degrees additively by its own bounded degree). (b) (Z/2)³ character splitting (D4): divides n by up to 8 and localizes the degree pathology per character.

**A4 (ordering the remaining alternatives).** Hermite form — only if you want unimodular certificates; more expensive than a kernel basis. Smith form — overkill, most expensive, rejected. Fraction-free (Bareiss) elimination — provably infeasible dense here (intermediate degrees ~6.6·10³ on a ~2.2·10³-square matrix is ~10¹⁴ bytes). ε-adic Newton at ε = 0 specifically — only if A(0) retains rank 2,208; otherwise it is A2 at a shifted point.

**A5 (physical reading of a genuinely large minimal degree).** If A1 returns D in the hundreds-to-thousands *after* A3 and the D-checks are clean, that is a theorem about this ansatz (support ⊗ alphabet ⊗ normalization), not yet about the block. Order of suspicion: (i) support truncation — enlarging the 64-monomial support preserves per-ε solvability and can only lower the minimal degree; this is precisely the mechanism by which a truncated ansatz stays pointwise-solvable while its minimal ε-degree explodes (the solution imitates the missing directions with high-degree ε-coefficients); A/B-test at the character-subproblem level where kernel bases cost minutes. (ii) Alphabet incompleteness, especially deck-orbit closure (D3), same mechanism. (iii) Per-grade normalization (D4). (iv) Only then "genuine" — and I know of no mechanism (statement of experience, not a theorem) by which a regular-singular dlog completion whose 88 siblings sit at degree 9–13 with the same Q-structure needs degree 10³. Before accepting genuineness, run D2: if the maximal cut of the coupled sectors contains a non-algebraic (K3) period, no alphabet completes this block to dlog and the degree blow-up is the geometry rejecting the ansatz — the right target becomes an ε-factorized non-dlog form (Dlapa–Henn–Wagner, arXiv:2211.16357; Görges–Nega–Tancredi–Wagner, arXiv:2305.14090; Pögel–Wang–Weinzierl, arXiv:2207.12893).

## B. Rationality of the triple covers

**B0 (structural expectation; proven modulo one computation).** Homogenize: each z_i² = P_i with P_i quadratic makes the projective model X ⊂ P⁵ an intersection of three quadrics, and a smooth (2,2,2) in P⁵ is a degree-8 K3 (adjunction: K = (2+2+2−6)H = 0). Internally to your data: the pair cover X₁₂ is an intersection of two quadrics in P⁴, i.e. a quartic del Pezzo with K = −H; the third root is a double cover of X₁₂ branched along X₁₂ ∩ {Q₃ = 0} ∈ |2H| = |−2K|, and a double cover of a surface branched in |−2K| has trivial canonical class — a K3 whenever the branch curve has at worst ADE singularities (nodal degenerations remain Kodaira dimension 0). For the record, your CF300 triple simplifies to P₁ = (1+v+w)² − 4w, P₂ = (1+v+w)² − 4v, P₃ = 1 − 4vw, and CF259 contains the Källén form λ(1,v,w) = (1−v−w)² − 4vw — classic phase-space quadrics; both triples fit the (2,2,2) picture. So the default expectation for both families is: minimal model K3 ⇒ irrational ⇒ no rational two-variable chart; and since unirational surfaces are rational in characteristic 0 (Castelnuovo), no weaker chart exists either — if K3 is confirmed, question B's global chart is *provably impossible*, which matches both your failure to find one and the direct precedent of Festi–van Straten, "Bhabha scattering and a special pencil of K3 surfaces," Commun. Num. Theor. Phys. 13 (2019), arXiv:1809.04970. The one escape hatch: your quadrics are rank-deficient (e.g., 4VU + W² is a rank-3 cone), and sufficiently bad (non-ADE, non-isolated) degeneration of the branch data can drop the Kodaira dimension — so compute, don't assume.

**B1 (decision procedure, days not weeks).** The birational class of X is independent of which pair you rationalize first [proven], so one classification settles the verdict; still compute all three pullbacks because chart quality differs if the answer is "rational". For each pair (ij): pull P_k back through your explicit chart, clear square factors, obtaining z² = R_ij(s,t); then (a) run RationalizeRoots on it (Besier–Wasser–Weinzierl, Comput. Phys. Commun. 253 (2020) 107197, arXiv:1910.13251), which implements the sufficient criteria of Besier–van Straten–Weinzierl, Commun. Num. Theor. Phys. 13 (2019) 253, arXiv:1809.10983 — success ends the story with a chart in hand; (b) on failure, decide properly: resolve the double plane branched along B_ij (numerator·denominator mod squares, line at infinity included for odd degree), compute q and P₂, and apply Castelnuovo's rationality criterion q = P₂ = 0 (Beauville, *Complex Algebraic Surfaces*, ch. V), or equivalently run Schicho's general surface-parametrization algorithm (J. Symb. Comput. 26 (1998) 1–29; Magma's parametrization machinery). Shortcuts: deg B ≤ 4 after minimization ⇒ rational (dP2 or better); smooth-or-ADE sextic ⇒ K3 ⇒ impossible; sextic with a point of multiplicity ≥ 4 ⇒ projection gives a conic bundle, decide via Iskovskikh's conic-bundle criteria (Iskovskikh 1967; 1987 survey). Budget: 2–5 days of Magma/Singular work total for both triples.

**B2 (adopt the partial chart regardless of the verdict).** Rationalize the best pair and carry the third root as a single quadratic extension z² = R(s,t) over F_p(s,t): 8 grades → 2, the hard block's gauge unknowns drop ~4× at fixed support, and all of your solved two-root technology transfers verbatim. Choose the pair by measurement, not principle: (i) minimal degree/height of R after square-clearing; (ii) minimal pullback degree of the 68 letters and of the physical threshold divisors; (iii) chart defined over the prime field. The one risk is pulled-back letter degrees inflating the support budget — calibrate once on an already-solved two-root sibling before refactoring. This is clearly better than the fully algebraic 8-grade frame for everything that remains, unless the pullback degrees blow up, which you will see immediately.

**B3 (context).** Non-rationalizability of the alphabet does *not* imply non-polylogarithmic integrals: Heller–von Manteuffel–Schabinger computed the two-loop mixed EW-QCD Drell–Yan integrals with an unrationalizable root system, polylogarithmic throughout (Phys. Rev. D 102 (2020) 016025, arXiv:1907.00491) — precedent for option (ii). General orientation: Weinzierl, *Feynman Integrals* (Springer 2022, arXiv:2201.03593), rationalization chapter; for the "geometry beyond rationalization" landscape, Bourjaily–McLeod–von Hippel–Wilhelm, Phys. Rev. Lett. 122 (2019) 031601, arXiv:1810.07689.

## C. Strategy for the three families

**C1 (primary): (ii) then (i).** Finish the dlog completion with algebraic letters and ε-dependent rational residues via the A-route, and defer ε-factorization to the family-level constant/rational rotation already in your pipeline — with the diagonal blocks certified in ε-form, this is the same endpoint as (i) through strictly smaller per-step systems, and it preserves uniformity with the 88 solved families. Add one pragmatic bypass: for NNLO you only ever need a fixed ε-depth, so if the exact minimal degree turns out large, a δ-adic series section around a generic ε₀ to the depth budget (a dozen lifting steps on the fixed minor, minutes) feeds the endpoint expansion, boundary matching, and assembly just as well; keep the exact-rational computation running for certification only. Effort: 1–2 weeks of integration plus hours-to-days of compute. Failure modes: D-checks expose a formulation defect (fix and rerun, cheap), or D2 exposes a new period (go to C4). Direct ε-form tools (Lee, JHEP 04 (2015) 108, arXiv:1411.0911; Meyer/CANONICA, Comput. Phys. Commun. 222 (2018) 295) don't apply off the shelf over the triple cover, which is another reason not to make (i) a separate first-class track.

**C2 (parallel insurance): (iii).** Transport the three families numerically on the *existing* exact system — no canonical form needed: series transport à la DiffExp (Hidding, Comput. Phys. Commun. 269 (2021) 107725, arXiv:2006.05510) or SeaSyde (Armadillo et al., Comput. Phys. Commun. 282 (2023) 108545), boundaries from AMFlow (Liu–Ma, Comput. Phys. Commun. 283 (2023) 108565, arXiv:2201.11669) or your exact boundary periods, endpoint expansions by transporting into the boundary layers (Moriello, JHEP 01 (2020) 150, arXiv:1907.13234). Effort: 2–4 person-weeks with mature tools, ~10²–10³ CPU-h for 40–60-digit grids. Failure modes: none structural — apparent singularities of the non-canonical system slow the stepper, and branch-locus crossings of the three roots need care. Even if C1 lands first, this is the mandatory independent validation of exactly the three families that bypassed the certified pipeline.

**C3 (conditional): (iv) as a decision, not a hunt.** Spend the 2–5 days of B1 and stop. If, against expectation, a rational triple chart exists, rerunning the cheap rational-chart machinery is a 1–2 week jackpot. If K3 is confirmed, record the proof — it upgrades "we failed to find a chart" to "no chart exists," worth a paragraph in the paper — and fall back to B2's partial chart for whatever work remains. Time-box hard; the prior is against you.

**C4 (contingency).** If D2 finds a genuine non-algebraic period in the maximal cuts, abandon dlog for these sectors and either target an ε-factorized non-dlog form directly (Dlapa–Henn–Wagner arXiv:2211.16357; Görges–Nega–Tancredi–Wagner arXiv:2305.14090; Pögel–Wang–Weinzierl JHEP 09 (2022) 062, arXiv:2207.12893, and Phys. Rev. Lett. 130 (2023), arXiv:2211.04292) or let C2 alone carry the phenomenology — for an NNLO hard function that is entirely respectable. I consider this branch unlikely for cut phase-space integrals at this order, but it is the one world in which pushing the module route harder is wasted, so buy the half-day of information first.

## D. Sanity checks, ranked by information per unit cost

**D1 (the one I would bet on; do it today).** All degree measurements sit on one fixed 37-point kinematic set, and 2,368 = 37·64 equations against 2,260 unknowns is only 4.8% overdetermined. Two distinct risks. First, generation method — this is a repo-level fact I could not verify: if the points were produced by a *parametrization* (e.g., a curve along which all three P_i are simultaneously squares, the natural trick for making the z_i evaluable in F_p), then every residual function vanishing on that curve is invisible, and the per-ε system can be exactly solvable while the functional problem is not; the fitted "solutions" would then show precisely your pathology — solvable at every ε, wild ε-dependence, no low-degree section. Schwartz–Zippel protection requires independently random points: rejection-sample (v,w) uniformly with χ(P_i) = +1 (acceptance ~1/8, trivial). Second, even if random, re-run with a fresh disjoint set and with 74 points: rank must stay 2,208 and the nullity must stay 52 — in particular the 16 ε-varying kernel directions must survive; any change means the solution manifold was partly artifact and every downstream degree statement inherited it. Cost: minutes at 4 s/image. Control experiment: re-measure one *solved* sibling at the same 5% overdetermination; if its section degrees inflate past the known 9–13, you have reproduced the artifact mechanism in vitro.

**D2 (half a day; gates everything).** Maximal-cut audit of sectors 9 and 12 on the triple cover: verify the leading singularities are algebraic (dlog-type, presumably rational·(P₁P₂P₃)^(−1/2) in grade (1,1,1)) and that Q matches them grade by grade. Tools: Baikov-representation cuts (Frellesvig–Papadopoulos, JHEP 04 (2017) 083, arXiv:1701.07356; Primo–Tancredi, Nucl. Phys. B 916 (2017) 94, arXiv:1610.08397); dlog-integrand construction (Henn–Mistlberger–Smirnov–Wasser, JHEP 04 (2020) 167, arXiv:2002.09492); UT-seed method (Dlapa–Henn–Yan, JHEP 05 (2020) 025, arXiv:2002.02340). If a non-algebraic period appears, stop pushing dlog (see C4); if all cuts are algebraic, you also get an independent per-grade check of Q, which feeds A3/D4.

**D3 (half a day, before the big kernel run).** Alphabet closure and divisor coverage. Apply the seven nontrivial deck involutions to each of the 20 algebraic letters and verify each image's dlog lies in the Z-span of the 68; 20 is consistent with full orbits (orbit sizes divide 8), but a truncated orbit is exactly the "missing letter" that keeps per-ε solvability while inflating the minimal ε-degree — the completion imitates the absent deck-conjugates with high-degree ε-coefficients. Pair-cover letter lists imported from the 13 solved two-root families are the natural source of truncated orbits. Separately, enumerate the polar divisors of the (k,j) input block and both diagonal connections and confirm each is covered by letter zeros/poles; since your gauge frame is polynomial in the support, new poles can only come from letters, so b's polar list is the complete requirement.

**D4 (a day; multiplies everything else).** Equivariant splitting and the grade cross-tab. If the letter set is deck-closed (after D3), the completion operator commutes with (Z/2)³; Fourier-transforming unknowns and equations over the group splits the 2,368×2,260 problem into eight ~296×283 character blocks. Payoffs: kernel bases per block cost minutes (enabling rapid alphabet/support A/B tests for A5), the degree pathology localizes to specific characters (if only z₃-odd characters inflate, that points at particular letters or normalization), and failure to split is itself a bug detector for the grade arithmetic of letter products. Also cross-tabulate the 1,076 constant vs 1,184 varying coordinates, and the 36 constant vs 16 varying kernel directions, against grade labels: clean alignment with grades would indicate a missing per-grade normalization Q^{m_g} — test integer shifts m_g by re-running the degree probe per character.

**D5 (cheap, clarifying).** Predict the kernel independently: the 36-dimensional constant kernel should coincide with the constant intertwiner space of the two ε-factorized diagonal blocks (solutions (S, R) of dS = ε(M_k S − S M_j) + Σ R_a dlog φ_a with constant S), countable grade by grade; a mismatch is a bookkeeping bug. Then compute the minimal indices δ₁…δ₁₆ of the ε-varying kernel of A alone (a homogeneous, cheaper kernel-basis run): their sizes immediately bracket where the solution degree can live, and identify which directions carry it.

**D6 (minor).** Your degree-64 exclusion at 76 images has only ~10 images of headroom over the 2·64+2 needed; on all re-probes take 200–300 images (≈ 20 min at 4 s) so exclusion statements carry factor-2 margins, and keep the two-prime and held-out-ε criteria you already use — adding a held-out kinematic set (D1) to the acceptance battery.

## Proven vs. suggested, and the impossibility flags

Proven, or overwhelming-probability given your data: a rational-in-ε solution exists with degree ≤ ~6.7·10³ (Cramer on a fixed minor), so "no rational representative" is false and only minimality is open; all 53 kernel minimal indices are similarly bounded, with 36 known to vanish; the birational class of the triple cover — hence the rationalizability verdict — is independent of which pair is rationalized first; in two variables, Castelnuovo makes unirational = rational, so a K3 verdict kills all chart variants at once; and fraction-free dense elimination is out of reach at this size. Conditionally provable within days: both triple covers are K3 (double cover of a dP4 branched in |−2K|; smooth (2,2,2) in P⁵), pending only the branch-singularity computation — if it lands, question B's global chart is provably impossible, with Festi–van Straten arXiv:1809.04970 as the precedent to cite. Suggested (predictions, not theorems): the minimal-kernel computation, run after D1/D3/D4 are clean, returns a section at sibling scale — the exact ε-constancy of the 1,076 untouched coordinates is the tell; the likeliest defects in order are kinematic-point structure, deck-incomplete alphabet, and per-grade normalization; and the physics will be polylogarithmic with algebraic letters, per the Drell–Yan precedent, so option (ii)+(i) with (iii) as parallel insurance is the efficient frontier.
