\title{From Master Integrals to Functions:\\
the Differential-Equation Pipeline of the NNLO Double-Real Channel\\[1ex]
\large Lecture notes, full version}
\author{prepared for Max Zhang}
\date{August 24, 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
These notes explain, from first principles, how the $347$ master
integrals of the double-real channel of $pp \to h+X$ at NNLO are turned
from abstract basis elements of an IBP reduction into explicit analytic
functions of the kinematics, expanded in the dimensional regulator
$\eps$.  The chain of ideas is: masters obey first-order linear
differential equations in the kinematic invariants
(Section~\ref{sec:de}); the equations organize into $91$
block-triangular \emph{family} systems whose diagonal blocks fall into
only $173$ equivalence \emph{classes} (Section~\ref{sec:classes}); each
class is brought to \emph{canonical ($\eps$-)form}, in which the
equations solve themselves order by order in $\eps$ as iterated
integrals (Sections~\ref{sec:epsform}--\ref{sec:finding}); square roots
of kinematic polynomials are tamed by rational \emph{charts} or, when no
chart exists, by working in the Galois algebra of the roots
(Section~\ref{sec:roots}); the general solution is \emph{transported}
through kinematic space as words in a dlog alphabet
(Section~\ref{sec:transport}); and the remaining integration constants
--- the \emph{boundary periods} --- are pinned down by a small set of
physical principles, of which only a handful require computing any new
integral (Section~\ref{sec:boundary}).  A final section explains why the
singular behaviour of the solutions near the phase-space endpoints is
encoded in the equations themselves and how it is recovered without
expanding it away (Section~\ref{sec:endpoint}).  Every concept is
defined before use and every mechanism is illustrated on an example
small enough to check by hand.
\end{abstract}

\section{The object of study}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Where the integrals come from}

The physical target is the NNLO hard function for single-inclusive
hadron production, $pp \to h + X$, in collinear factorization.  The
double-real channel is the contribution in which two additional
partons are radiated into the final state and integrated over.  Its
building blocks are therefore \emph{phase-space integrals} of squared
tree amplitudes: integrals of rational functions of momenta over the
momenta of two unobserved massless particles, subject to on-shell
delta functions.

By \emph{reverse unitarity} each on-shell delta function is traded for
the discontinuity of a propagator,
\begin{equation}
  \delta^{(+)}(p^2) \;\longrightarrow\;
  \frac{1}{2\pi i}\left[\frac{1}{p^2 - i0} - \frac{1}{p^2 + i0}\right],
\end{equation}
which converts every phase-space integral into a (cut) two-loop
integral.  The point of this trade is purely structural: cut integrals
obey the same integration-by-parts (IBP) identities as ordinary loop
integrals, so the entire technology of loop reduction applies.  The
``cut'' lines are bookkept throughout --- an integral in which a cut
propagator has been cancelled is zero, and this rule propagates through
every identity below.

After projection onto the twist-two correlators, the hard functions
depend on two dimensionless Mandelstam ratios.  In these notes we call
them $(v,w)$, with physical region
\begin{equation}
  0 < v,\qquad 0 < w,\qquad v + w < 1 ,
  \label{eq:region}
\end{equation}
and everything is computed in dimensional regularization,
$d = 4 - 2\eps$.  (In the package the same two variables appear as
$x,y$ at the amplitude stage and as $v,w$ at the master stage; the
letters $x,y$ are reserved at the master stage for \emph{chart}
variables, Section~\ref{sec:roots}.)

\subsection{What ``solving the masters'' means}

IBP reduction expresses every integral of the problem as a
$\Q(v,w,\eps)$-linear combination of a finite basis of \emph{master
integrals}; for the double-real channel there are
\begin{equation}
  347 \ \text{masters}.
\end{equation}
The reduction is exact but purely algebraic: it tells us nothing about
the \emph{values} of the masters.  ``Solving'' them means producing,
for each master $I(v,w;\eps)$, a Laurent expansion
\begin{equation}
  I(v,w;\eps) \;=\; \sum_{k \ge k_0} \eps^{k}\, I^{(k)}(v,w),
\end{equation}
in which every coefficient $I^{(k)}$ is an explicit, evaluatable
function --- in practice a $\Q$-linear combination of \emph{iterated
integrals} (generalized polylogarithms) of the kinematics with exactly
known constant prefactors --- to a depth $k$ dictated by where the
master enters the cross section.  The rest of these notes is the story
of how one gets there.  The route has five conceptual stations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item derive first-order differential equations in $(v,w)$ for the
  masters (Section~\ref{sec:de});
\item organize them into families and classes so the hard work is done
  once per class, not once per master (Section~\ref{sec:classes});
\item transform to canonical $\eps$-form, the representation in which
  the equations integrate themselves (Sections~\ref{sec:epsform},
  \ref{sec:finding}, \ref{sec:roots});
\item integrate: build the general solution as words in a dlog
  alphabet (Section~\ref{sec:transport});
\item fix the integration constants from physics
  (Section~\ref{sec:boundary}) and extract the endpoint behaviour
  without destroying it (Section~\ref{sec:endpoint}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{From IBP to differential equations}
\label{sec:de}

\subsection{Why masters obey differential equations}

Fix an \emph{integral family}: a maximal set of inverse propagators
$D_1,\dots,D_N$ (including the cut ones) such that every scalar
product of loop and external momenta is a linear combination of the
$D_i$ and the external invariants.  The family's integrals are
\begin{equation}
  I_{a_1\dots a_N}(v,w;\eps)
  \;=\; \int \prod_{\ell=1}^{2}\frac{\dd^d k_\ell}{i\pi^{d/2}}\;
  \frac{1}{D_1^{a_1}\cdots D_N^{a_N}},
  \qquad a_i \in \Z ,
\end{equation}
with the cut lines fixed at power one and never cancelled.  Two facts
combine into the differential equations:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Differentiation stays inside the family.}  A derivative
  $\partial_v$ acting on the integrand can be written, by the chain
  rule, as a combination of derivatives with respect to external
  momenta contracted into the loop integrand.  Each such derivative
  raises or lowers propagator powers: it produces integrals
  $I_{a_1'\dots a_N'}$ \emph{of the same family}.
\item \textbf{IBP closes the system.}  Every integral of the family
  reduces to the finite master basis
  $\vec I = (I_1,\dots,I_n)^{\mathsf T}$ of that family and of its
  \emph{subsectors} (families obtained by deleting propagators).
\end{enumerate}
Applying (1) then (2) to each master gives a closed linear system
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \partial_v \vec I = A_v(v,w;\eps)\,\vec I,
  \qquad
  \partial_w \vec I = A_w(v,w;\eps)\,\vec I,
  \;}
  \label{eq:desys}
\end{equation}
with $A_v, A_w$ matrices of rational functions of $(v,w,\eps)$.  We
package the pair as a \emph{connection} one-form
$A = A_v\,\dd v + A_w\,\dd w$ and write $\dd \vec I = A\,\vec I$.

\begin{example}[The one-mass bubble, done by hand]
\label{ex:bubble}
The mechanism in miniature, on the classic one-loop example.  Let
\begin{equation}
  B(s;\eps) = \int \frac{\dd^d k}{i\pi^{d/2}}
  \frac{1}{k^2\,(k+p)^2}, \qquad s = p^2 .
\end{equation}
The family has $N=2$ propagators, $D_1 = k^2$, $D_2 = (k+p)^2$.  Its
only nontrivial master is $B$ itself; its subsector (delete $D_2$) is
the massless tadpole, which vanishes.  Differentiate:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial B}{\partial s}
  = \frac{1}{2s}\, p^\mu \frac{\partial B}{\partial p^\mu}
  = -\frac{1}{2s}\int \frac{\dd^d k}{i\pi^{d/2}}
    \frac{2p\cdot(k+p)}{k^2\,[(k+p)^2]^2}.
\end{equation}
Write the numerator in terms of inverse propagators,
$2 p\cdot(k+p) = (k+p)^2 - k^2 + s = D_2 - D_1 + s$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial B}{\partial s}
  = -\frac{1}{2s}\Big[ I_{1,1} - I_{2,0} + s\, I_{1,2} \Big] ,
\end{equation}
where $I_{1,1}=B$, $I_{2,0}=0$ (scaleless), and $I_{1,2}$ is reduced by
the standard IBP identity
$0=\int \dd^d k\, \partial_{k^\mu} [k^\mu/(D_1 D_2)]$, which yields
$I_{1,2} = -\tfrac{d-3}{s} I_{1,1}$.  Substituting,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial B}{\partial s}
  = -\frac{1}{2s}\Big[B + (d-3)B\Big]
  = -\,\frac{1-\eps}{s}\, B
  \qquad (d = 4-2\eps).
  \label{eq:bubblede}
\end{equation}
A one-line first-order equation whose solution is
$B \propto s^{-\eps}\,s^{\,0}\cdot s^{-1+\dots}$ --- more precisely
$B = c(\eps)\, (-s)^{-\eps}/s^{\,0}$ up to normalization: the equation
fixes the full $s$-dependence, $B(s) = c(\eps)(-s)^{-\eps}\cdot
s^{-(1-\eps)+\eps}$; carrying out the algebra,
$B(s) = c(\eps)\,(-s)^{-\eps}$ with
$c(\eps)= \Gamma(1+\eps)\Gamma(1-\eps)^2/\big(\eps(1-2\eps)\Gamma(1-2\eps)\big)$
fixed by one direct evaluation (the ``boundary constant'').  Every
ingredient of the full problem --- differentiation into the family,
IBP closure, a first-order system, one undetermined constant --- is
already visible here.
\end{example}

\subsection{Block-triangular structure}

Order the sectors of a family by the number of propagators.  A
derivative of a master never generates a sector with \emph{more}
propagators than appear in the master's own sector plus what IBP can
produce, and reduction only moves \emph{down} into subsectors.
Consequently, if the master vector is ordered subsectors-first, the
connection is \emph{block lower triangular}:
\begin{equation}
  A \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    a_{11} & & & \\
    a_{21} & a_{22} & & \\
    \vdots & & \ddots & \\
    a_{b1} & a_{b2} & \cdots & a_{bb}
  \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:blocktriangular}
\end{equation}
where each diagonal block $a_{ss}$ couples only the masters of one
strongly connected sector group, and the off-diagonal blocks feed
lower sectors into higher ones.  This shape drives the entire
strategy: diagonal blocks are the irreducible analytic content;
off-diagonal blocks are ``plumbing'' that can be attached
afterwards (Section~\ref{sec:finding}).

\subsection{Integrability}

Because $\partial_v\partial_w \vec I = \partial_w \partial_v \vec I$,
the two matrices in Eq.~\eqref{eq:desys} are not independent: they
must satisfy the \emph{flatness} (integrability) condition
\begin{equation}
  \partial_v A_w - \partial_w A_v + [A_v, A_w] = 0,
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \dd A - A \wedge A = 0 .
  \label{eq:flatness}
\end{equation}
Flatness is used in three ways in the pipeline: as a stringent
correctness check on every derived or transformed connection; as the
guarantee that solutions can be continued along \emph{any} path in the
$(v,w)$ region with a path-independent result (Section~\ref{sec:transport});
and as a labour saver --- once flatness of a source system is certified,
the flatness of any exactly gauge-equivalent system follows and need
not be recomputed from scratch.

\section{Families, blocks, and the 173 classes}
\label{sec:classes}

\subsection{Canonical families}

\begin{definition}[Integral family, canonical family]
An \emph{integral family} is a set of inverse propagators as above.
Two families are \emph{equivalent} if a loop-momentum redefinition
$k_\ell \to M_{\ell m} k_m + (\text{external})$ with
$M \in GL(2,\Z)$, $|\det M|=1$, maps one propagator set bijectively
onto the other while sending cut lines to cut lines.  Such shifts
leave the integration measure invariant, so equivalent families have
literally equal integrals up to index relabeling.  A \emph{canonical
family} is a representative of an equivalence class, given a stable
name \texttt{CF}$n$.
\end{definition}

This is the ``momentum shifting'' one half-remembers.  Detection does
not search over shifts: a family is characterized, up to exactly these
shifts, by its Symanzik polynomials, and Pak's canonical ordering makes
equivalence a hash comparison; each detected equivalence is then
\emph{verified} by explicitly constructing the shift.  At NNLO
double-real this merges $1898$ raw families into $430$ canonical ones,
of which $374$ carry reduction targets, and of \emph{those} the
$91$ families listed below actually carry masters.

Because differentiation is family-local
(Section~\ref{sec:de}), the differential-equation problem splits into
one independent system per master-carrying canonical family:
\begin{equation}
  347 \ \text{masters} \;=\; \bigsqcup_{n} \ \vec I_{\texttt{CF}n},
  \qquad 91 \ \text{systems of dimension } 2 \text{ to } 47 .
\end{equation}
``Forming the families of differential equations'' is therefore not an
extra construction: the families were fixed at reduction time, and the
DE inherits their boundaries.  What has to be \emph{built} per family
is the pair $(A_v, A_w)$, by differentiating each master and reducing,
and the certified statement that Eq.~\eqref{eq:flatness} holds.

\subsection{Blocks and classes}

\begin{definition}[Block]
A \emph{block} of a family system is a strongly connected component of
the dependency graph of its connection: a minimal group of masters
that appear in each other's derivatives.  Ordered by sectors, the
blocks are exactly the diagonal blocks $a_{ss}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:blocktriangular}.  The $91$ family systems contain
$1119$ blocks in total.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Class]
\label{def:class}
Two blocks (possibly from different families) are \emph{equivalent}
if their connections are related by a permutation of the basis,
optionally composed with the kinematic involution $v \leftrightarrow
w$, as an \emph{exact rational matrix identity}.  A \emph{class} is an
equivalence class of blocks; the double-real inventory has $173$
classes.  Each class carries a representative block and, once solved,
a stored $\eps$-form for that representative.
\end{definition}

Why do so many of the $1119$ blocks coincide?  Physically: a block is
the differential system of one subtopology's masters, and the same
subtopology occurs inside many parent families --- the momentum-shift
merging happened at the level of \emph{complete} families, but
sub-diagrams recur far more often than whole diagrams.  The involution
$v \leftrightarrow w$ is the crossing $t \leftrightarrow u$ of the
underlying process, which maps cut diagrams to cut diagrams.
Equivalence is decided by exact identity of matrices, so no physics
argument is ever \emph{trusted}: a candidate permutation-plus-swap
either reproduces the representative entry by entry or the blocks are
not identified.

The economics of the class decomposition is the point: instead of
canonicalizing $1119$ blocks, one canonicalizes $173$ representatives
--- and the many-page labour of Sections~\ref{sec:finding} and
\ref{sec:roots} happens $173$ times, not $1119$ and not $347$.
The map back (which family rows realize which class, with which
permutation and swap) is bookkept in a registry and re-verified when
the family is assembled.

\section{The canonical ($\eps$-)form and why it solves the equations}
\label{sec:epsform}

\subsection{Definition}

\begin{definition}[$\eps$-form, letters, alphabet, residues]
A family (or block) system is in \emph{canonical form}, or
\emph{$\eps$-form}, if its connection is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \dd \vec F \;=\; \eps \sum_{a} R_a \,\dd\log \varphi_a(v,w)\;\vec F,
  \;}
  \label{eq:epsform}
\end{equation}
where the $R_a$ are \emph{constant} rational matrices (the
\emph{residues}) and the $\varphi_a$ are polynomials (or algebraic
functions, Section~\ref{sec:roots}) of the kinematics, free of $\eps$:
the \emph{letters}.  The set $\{\varphi_a\}$ is the \emph{alphabet}.
The regulator appears exactly once, as an overall prefactor.
\end{definition}

For a real specimen: the certified family \texttt{CF12} (a
$14$-master system) has the six-letter alphabet
\begin{equation}
  \{\, v,\; w,\; 1-v,\; 1-w,\; v+w,\; 1-v-w \,\}
\end{equation}
(written in its chart variables in the record), and every entry of its
connection is $\eps$ times a $\Q$-linear combination of
$\dd\log$'s of these six polynomials.  Harder families have richer
alphabets --- \texttt{CF231}'s includes quadratic letters like
$1+2s+s^2+y-6sy+s^2 y$ in its chart variables --- but the shape
\eqref{eq:epsform} is identical.

Three immediate consequences of the shape:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fuchsian singularities.}  The connection has only
  logarithmic (simple-pole) singularities, located exactly on the zero
  loci $\varphi_a = 0$.  No higher poles, no poles hidden at
  $\eps$-dependent locations.
\item \textbf{Manifest integrability.}  Since each
  $\dd\log\varphi_a$ is closed, flatness \eqref{eq:flatness} reduces
  to the constant-matrix condition
  $\sum_{a<b}[R_a,R_b]\,\dd\log\varphi_a\wedge\dd\log\varphi_b = 0$
  restricted to the algebraic relations among the letters --- checked
  once, on constants.
\item \textbf{Uniform weight.}  Solutions normalized at a point are,
  order by order in $\eps$, iterated integrals of uniform
  \emph{transcendental weight} $k$ at order $\eps^k$ (weight $=$
  number of integrations; $\log$ has weight 1, $\mathrm{Li}_2$ weight
  2, $\pi^2$ weight 2, \dots).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why the $\eps$-form integrates itself}

Write $\hat A = \sum_a R_a \dd\log\varphi_a$, so $\dd\vec F = \eps
\hat A\,\vec F$ with $\hat A$ independent of $\eps$.  Expand
$\vec F = \sum_{k\ge 0}\eps^k \vec F^{(k)}$ and match powers:
\begin{equation}
  \dd \vec F^{(0)} = 0,
  \qquad
  \dd \vec F^{(k)} = \hat A\, \vec F^{(k-1)} \quad (k \ge 1).
  \label{eq:orderbyorder}
\end{equation}
The system has become a \emph{recursion of quadratures}: the zeroth
order is constant; every next order is obtained by integrating known
functions.  Integrating from a base point $p_0$ along a path
$\gamma$,
\begin{equation}
  \vec F^{(k)}(p) = \vec F^{(k)}(p_0) +
  \int_\gamma \hat A\; \vec F^{(k-1)},
\end{equation}
and unfolding the recursion produces \emph{Chen iterated integrals}
\begin{equation}
  \vec F(p) \;=\; \Pexp\!\Big(\eps \int_{\gamma} \hat A\Big)\,
  \vec F(p_0)
  \;=\; \sum_{k\ge0} \eps^k
  \sum_{a_1,\dots,a_k}
  R_{a_k}\!\cdots R_{a_1}
  \underbrace{\int_{0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_k\le 1}
  \prod_{i}\dd\log \varphi_{a_i}(\gamma(t_i))}_{
  \text{a weight-}k\text{ iterated integral}}\;
  \vec F(p_0).
  \label{eq:pexp}
\end{equation}
Flatness makes the answer independent of the path within a fixed
homotopy class.  The analytic problem is thereby \emph{finished} up to
two data: the alphabet functions along paths (a bookkeeping problem,
Section~\ref{sec:transport}) and the boundary vector $\vec F(p_0)$ (a
physics problem, Section~\ref{sec:boundary}).  This, in one sentence,
is why we want the $\eps$-form: \emph{it converts a coupled system of
PDEs with $\eps$ entangled in every coefficient into a triangular
tower of explicit integrals whose $\eps$-dependence is purely
book-kept by the expansion order.}

Contrast the generic situation: if $A$ depends on $\eps$ non-trivially,
the orders of the $\eps$-expansion of $\vec I$ do not decouple ---
$\dd \vec I^{(k)}$ involves $\vec I^{(k)}$ itself through the
$\eps^0$ part of $A$, so one must solve a genuine coupled ODE system
at every order, with no uniform structure and no control over the
function space of the answer.

\begin{example}[A $1\times1$ warm-up]
$\dd F = \eps\,\dd\log w\,F$ integrates to
$F = C\,w^{\eps} = C\,(1 + \eps\log w + \tfrac{\eps^2}{2}\log^2 w +
\dots)$: the recursion \eqref{eq:orderbyorder} generates the
logarithm tower of the exact power $w^\eps$.  Note the two readings of
the same object: \emph{expanded}, it is a polynomial in $\log w$ at
each order; \emph{unexpanded}, it is a power $w^\eps$ whose exponent
is the (here $1\times1$) residue times $\eps$.  Both readings are
needed later (Section~\ref{sec:endpoint}).
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A $2\times2$ system producing a dilogarithm]
\label{ex:li2}
Take the alphabet $\{w,\,1-w\}$ and
\begin{equation}
  \dd \vec F = \eps\left[
  \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0\\ 0 & -1\end{pmatrix}\dd\log w
  +
  \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0\\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\dd\log(1-w)
  \right]\vec F,
  \qquad
  \vec F(w_0{=}0^+)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
(boundary chosen regular at $w=0$; the precise meaning of ``value at a
singular point'' is Section~\ref{sec:endpoint}).  Order by order for
the second component $F_2$:
\begin{align}
  F_2^{(1)}(w) &= \int_0^w \dd\log(1-w')\; F_1^{(0)}
               = \log(1-w) ,\\
  F_2^{(2)}(w) &= \int_0^w \Big[\dd\log(1-w')\,F_1^{(1)}
               \;-\;\dd\log w' \,F_2^{(1)}\Big]
               = -\int_0^w \frac{\dd w'}{w'}\,\log(1-w')
               = \mathrm{Li}_2(w) ,
\end{align}
using $F_1^{(k)} = \delta_{k0}$ (the first row is decoupled here).  The
weight-2 dilogarithm appears exactly as the two-fold iterated integral
with the word $(w,\,1-w)$; at order $\eps^3$ the same mechanics
produces $\mathrm{Li}_3$ and products, and so on.  Every master in the
project is, at each order, a $\Q$-combination of precisely such words
in its family's alphabet.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Existence]
Nothing guarantees a rational $\eps$-form exists for a given block.
For the double-real inventory it does exist for every one of the $173$
classes --- but for some only after a change of variables that
rationalizes a square root (Section~\ref{sec:roots}), and for the
three-root families the question is precisely the current frontier.
The working attitude: the $\eps$-form is a \emph{certificate format},
and any method whatsoever may be used to find the transformation, as
long as the final identity is checked exactly.
\end{remark}

\section{Finding the transformation to $\eps$-form}
\label{sec:finding}

The unknown is an invertible matrix $T(v,w;\eps)$, the \emph{gauge
transformation}, such that the transformed connection
\begin{equation}
  A' \;=\; T^{-1} A\, T \;-\; T^{-1}\dd T
  \label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
has the form \eqref{eq:epsform}.  (Check the transformation law:
if $\dd\vec I = A\vec I$ and $\vec I = T\vec F$, then
$\dd(T\vec F) = \dd T\,\vec F + T\,\dd\vec F = A\,T\vec F$, i.e.
$\dd\vec F = (T^{-1}AT - T^{-1}\dd T)\vec F$.)  The strategy exploits
the block triangle \eqref{eq:blocktriangular}: first each
\emph{diagonal} block (once per class), then the \emph{off-diagonal}
completion (once per family), then a final constant-in-$(v,w)$
transformation that concentrates left-over $\eps$-dependence.

\subsection{Diagonal blocks: normal forms and balances}
\label{sec:diagonal}

For the diagonal blocks two classical ideas suffice (implemented by
the CANONICA-style rational ansatz and the Lee/Libra reduction; both
are used in the project as \emph{candidate generators}, with exact
verification as the only acceptance).

\paragraph{(i) Rational ansatz.}  Parametrize $T$ as a matrix of
rational functions with denominators built from an educated guess of
the alphabet and numerators of bounded degree with unknown
$\eps$-dependent coefficients; demand \eqref{eq:gauge} take the form
\eqref{eq:epsform}; solve the resulting linear system for the
coefficients.  Blunt but complete at fixed degree.

\paragraph{(ii) Fuchsification and balances.}  First reduce all poles
of the connection to simple poles (\emph{Fuchsian} form), then adjust
residue eigenvalues to be proportional to $\eps$, using
\emph{balance transformations}
\begin{equation}
  T = 1 + \frac{c}{v - v_1}\,P
  \qquad (P^2 = P \ \text{a projector}),
\end{equation}
which shift eigenvalues of the residue at $v_1$ by integers while
touching no other singular point.  A sequence of balances walks all
eigenvalues into the normalized window, after which a constant
transformation splits off the overall $\eps$.

\begin{example}[Removing a double pole by hand]
\label{ex:fuchs}
Consider the scalar equation
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd F}{\dd w} = \left(\frac{\alpha}{w^2} +
  \frac{\beta\,\eps}{w}\right) F .
\end{equation}
The $w^{-2}$ term is non-Fuchsian.  Substitute $F = e^{-\alpha/w} G$
(an ``exponential integrating factor'', the scalar shadow of a
balance):
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd G}{\dd w} = \frac{\beta\eps}{w}\, G
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  G = C\,w^{\beta\eps} .
\end{equation}
In the multi-master world the analogous statement is that an
apparent higher pole sits in a nilpotent or removable direction and a
rational $T$ eliminates it; when it does \emph{not} (a genuine
irregular singularity), no $\eps$-form exists in those variables ---
which for our inventory never happens after the correct chart is
chosen.
\end{example}

\subsection{Off-diagonal completion: the strip equation}
\label{sec:strips}

Assume all diagonal blocks are already in $\eps$-form (their class
transformations having been inserted), and let the connection be
block-triangular with $\eps$-form diagonal blocks
$a_{kk} = \eps\, e_k$ and non-canonical couplings $b_{kj}$
($j<k$).  Complete one row at a time with the unipotent gauge
\begin{equation}
  T = 1 + D, \qquad D \ \text{supported on block row } k,\
  \text{columns } j < k .
\end{equation}
Because $D$ is strictly block-triangular, $D^2 = 0$ and $T^{-1} = 1 -
D$, so \eqref{eq:gauge} gives \emph{exactly} (no expansion)
\begin{equation}
  A' = A + A D - D A - \dd D .
\end{equation}
The $(k,j)$ block of the requirement ``$A'$ is in $\eps$-form'' reads
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \dd D_{kj}
  = \eps\big( e_k\, D_{kj} - D_{kj}\, c_j \big)
  + \bar b_{kj}
  - \eps \sum_a K_a\, \dd\log\varphi_a ,
  \;}
  \label{eq:strip}
\end{equation}
where $c_j$ is the (already canonical) diagonal block of column $j$,
$\bar b_{kj} = b_{kj} - \sum_{j<m<k} D_{km}\, b_{mj}$ collects the
original coupling and the feed-through of previously completed blocks
of the same row, and the constant matrices $K_a$ are unknowns on the
same footing as $D_{kj}$: they become the residues of the completed
block.  Equation \eqref{eq:strip} is a \emph{linear} PDE for the pair
$(D_{kj}, K_a)$ --- linearity is what makes the whole completion
tractable.

\begin{example}[A $2\times2$ completion by hand]
\label{ex:strip}
Alphabet $\{v, 1{+}v\}$, one variable for brevity.  Suppose
\begin{equation}
  A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0\\[2pt]
  \dfrac{1}{1+v} & \eps\,\dd\log v \cdot 1\end{pmatrix}
  \quad\text{schematically:}\quad
  e = \dd\log v,\ \ c = 0,\ \ b = \frac{\dd v}{1+v}.
\end{equation}
The coupling $b$ carries no $\eps$, so it cannot be a residue term;
try $D$ rational in $v$ with $K_{1+v}$ constant:
\begin{equation}
  D' = \eps\,\frac{D}{v} + \frac{1}{1+v} - \eps \frac{K}{1+v}.
\end{equation}
At order $\eps^0$: $D_0' = \frac{1}{1+v}$ would force $D_0 =
\log(1+v)$ --- not rational.  The resolution is the standard one: relax
the target by allowing the completed block to keep an
$\eps$-independent \emph{residue} piece only if it is a pure dlog with
constant coefficient --- here it is ($b = \dd\log(1+v)$!), so the
correct move is $D = 0$, $K$ absorbing at order $\eps^0$:
the block $b$ is \emph{already} $\dd\log(1+v)$ with constant residue
$1$, and a subsequent \emph{constant} $\eps$-dependent transformation
(next subsection) rescales the row to pull the residue into the
$\eps\,R_a$ shape.  The example teaches the two mechanisms in the
production system: rational $D$ absorbs what is absorbable; constant
residues $K_a$ and the final constant $T(\eps)$ absorb the rest.
\end{example}

\subsection{Solving the strip equation over finite fields}
\label{sec:ff}

For real blocks ($D_{kj}$ up to $4\times 24$ entries of rational
functions with numerators of high degree) Eq.~\eqref{eq:strip} is
solved by \emph{ansatz plus modular linear algebra}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Ansatz.}  $D_{kj} = N(v,w;\eps)/\prod_a
  \varphi_a^{m_a}$ with the denominator built from the letters and the
  numerator a polynomial with unknown coefficients; the support (which
  monomials) is bounded by a certified degree census of the forcing
  $\bar b_{kj}$.
\item \textbf{Sampling.}  Substitute many rational points
  $(v_i,w_i)$ and regulator values $\eps_j$, all reduced modulo a
  $31$-bit prime $p$: each sample turns \eqref{eq:strip} into exact
  linear equations over $\Fp$ for the unknown coefficients.  Solve;
  repeat over several primes and enough samples.
\item \textbf{Reconstruction.}  Chinese-remainder the per-prime images
  and lift each coefficient from $\Z/p_1p_2\cdots$ back to $\Q$ by
  rational reconstruction; validate on samples never used in the fit
  and at a fresh prime.
\item \textbf{Exact certificate.}  The reconstructed $(D,K)$ is
  substituted back into \eqref{eq:strip} \emph{symbolically} (or into
  the equivalent whole-family identity at the end): only this exact
  identity, never the statistics of the fit, is the acceptance.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{example}[Rational reconstruction in one line]
Modulo $p=101$: suppose a fitted coefficient is $29 \pmod{101}$.
The lattice reduction behind rational reconstruction finds the
smallest fraction congruent to it: $29 \equiv \tfrac{1}{7} \pmod{101}$
(since $7\cdot29=203=2\cdot101+1$).  With a second prime,
$p=103$: $\tfrac17 \equiv 59\pmod{103}$; CRT of $(29,59)$ over
$101\cdot103$ reconstructs to $\tfrac17$ again, and agreement of the
lift across growing moduli (plus a fresh-prime check) is the
statistical evidence --- the proof remains the final symbolic
identity.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Gauge freedom and the choice of representative]
\label{rem:representative}
When the linear system for $(D,K)$ has a nullspace, the completed
block is not unique: any solution differs from another by
$\eps$-form-preserving redefinitions.  All representatives are
\emph{correct}, but they differ wildly in the \emph{degrees} of the
rational functions they propagate into later rows --- and degree
growth compounds row by row.  The production rule fixes the
representative by pinning the coefficients of the \emph{highest}
allowed numerator monomials to zero (leaving the constant dlog
residues free), which empirically selects the low-degree solution.
This is a pure engineering choice invisible in the final certified
$\eps$-form, but it decides whether a family solves in minutes or in
an hour.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The final constant transformation}
\label{sec:constT}

After the block completion, the connection is a dlog form whose
residue matrices may still depend on $\eps$ (rational functions of
$\eps$ times dlogs; the letters themselves are $\eps$-free).  The last
step is a transformation $T(\eps)$ that is \emph{constant in the
kinematics}: then $\dd T = 0$ and \eqref{eq:gauge} is a pure
conjugation $A' = T^{-1} A T$, acting letter by letter on the
residues.  One asks for $T(\eps)$ with
\begin{equation}
  T^{-1}(\eps)\, \tilde R_a(\eps)\, T(\eps) = \eps\, R_a
  \quad\text{simultaneously for all } a,
\end{equation}
a finite-dimensional problem solved by sampling the connection at a
few exact rational kinematic points and factoring the $\eps$-dependence
of the resulting constant matrices; the found $T(\eps)$ is then
verified by the exact symbolic identity on the full connection.  Why a
constant $T$ can suffice: the offending $\eps$-dependence originates
from $\eps$-dependent normalizations of the master basis (each
sector's masters were normalized with $\eps$-dependent prefactors),
i.e.\ from a basis choice --- and basis choices are undone by constant
matrices.

\section{Square roots, charts, and the Galois structure of the roots}
\label{sec:roots}

\subsection{Where the roots come from}

Nothing so far required the letters to be polynomials in $(v,w)$ ---
and for some blocks they are not.  The origin is the geometry of the
$3$-particle phase space.  For three massless final-state momenta with
fixed invariants, the physical region is bounded by the vanishing of a
Gram determinant, which in our variables is the K\"all\'en polynomial
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_1(v,w) \;=\; (1 - v - w)^2 - 4\,v\,w .
  \label{eq:kallen}
\end{equation}
(The name: for the triangle function
$\lambda(a,b,c)=a^2+b^2+c^2-2ab-2bc-2ca$ one has
$\lambda_1(v,w) = \lambda(1,v,w)$.)  Masters whose sectors probe this
boundary satisfy equations whose solutions have branch points on
$\lambda_1 = 0$ --- and since \emph{square-root} branch points cannot
be produced by dlogs of polynomials, their alphabets necessarily
contain the algebraic letter $\sqrt{\lambda_1}$, typically through
odd combinations like
$\dd\log\frac{1-v-w-\sqrt{\lambda_1}}{1-v-w+\sqrt{\lambda_1}}$.
The double-real inventory realizes five independent radicands:
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_1 = (1{-}v{-}w)^2 {-} 4vw,\quad
  \lambda_2 = \lambda_1(-v,w),\quad
  \lambda_3 = \lambda_1(v,-w),\quad
  4v + w^2,\quad v^2 + 4w,\quad 1 - 4vw
  \label{eq:radicands}
\end{equation}
(six expressions; $\lambda_2$/$\lambda_3$ and the two quartic-type ones
are exchanged by $v\leftrightarrow w$).

\begin{definition}[Number of roots of a family]
The \emph{root set} of a family (or block) is the set of independent
square classes among the radicands appearing in its alphabet ---
radicands counted modulo perfect-square factors and modulo
multiplicative combinations (e.g.\ $\sqrt{q_1}$, $\sqrt{q_2}$,
$\sqrt{q_1 q_2}$ count as \emph{two} roots, because the third is a
product of the others).  ``A two-root family'' means its alphabet
involves two independent such roots.  The inventory:
$31$ one-root families, $13$ two-root, $3$ three-root
(\texttt{CF259}, \texttt{CF300}, \texttt{CF303}); the remaining $44$
are root-free (``zero-root'').
\end{definition}

The root count is \emph{measured}, not guessed: radicands are read off
the derived connection and reduced to square classes exactly.  It is
the single number that decides which solving technology applies.

\subsection{Charts: rationalizing one root}

\begin{definition}[Chart]
A \emph{chart} is a rational change of variables
$(v,w) = \big(f(x,y),\, g(x,y)\big)$ with non-degenerate Jacobian,
such that a designated radicand becomes a perfect square:
$q\big(f,g\big) = r(x,y)^2$ with $r$ rational.  In the chart, the
root is the rational function $r$, and a block whose alphabet involves
only $\sqrt q$ acquires a \emph{rational} $\eps$-form in $(x,y)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Deriving the K\"all\'en chart]
\label{ex:kallenchart}
Seek $(v,w)=(f,g)$ with $\lambda_1(f,g)$ a square.  Try the product
ansatz suggested by the factorized boundary:
\begin{equation}
  v = x\,y, \qquad w = (1-x)(1-y).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
  1 - v - w &= 1 - xy - (1-x)(1-y) = x + y - 2xy,\\
  \lambda_1 &= (x+y-2xy)^2 - 4\,xy\,(1{-}x)(1{-}y)
  = x^2 + y^2 - 2xy = (x-y)^2 .
\end{align}
So in this chart $\sqrt{\lambda_1} = x - y$, the Jacobian determinant
is $\partial(v,w)/\partial(x,y) = x - y$ (nonzero away from the
diagonal), and every odd-root letter becomes rational, e.g.
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1-v-w-\sqrt{\lambda_1}}{1-v-w+\sqrt{\lambda_1}}
  = \frac{(x+y-2xy)-(x-y)}{(x+y-2xy)+(x-y)}
  = \frac{y(1-x)\,}{x(1-y)}\cdot\frac{2}{2}
  \quad\text{--- a ratio of polynomial letters.}
\end{equation}
This is the chart \textsf{Kallen1}; \textsf{Kallen2}/\textsf{3} are
the sign variants $v = \pm xy$, $w=\mp(1-x)(1-y)$ rationalizing
$\lambda_{2,3}$; \textsf{Q4a/b} handle $4v+w^2$ and $v^2+4w$ by conic
parametrization; \textsf{Bilinear115} handles $1-4vw$.
\end{example}

The systematic origin of such charts: $q(v,w) = r^2$ defines a conic
(in suitable projective coordinates), and a conic with one rational
point is rationally parametrized by the pencil of lines through that
point --- the same construction that parametrizes Pythagorean triples.
The catalog charts are precomputed instances of this construction,
stored with their substitution, root image, Jacobian, and an exact
verification that $q\circ\text{subst} = r^2$.

Working in a chart costs one \emph{pull-back} of the whole family
connection by the chain rule,
\begin{equation}
  A_x = A_v\,\partial_x f + A_w\,\partial_x g,
  \qquad
  A_y = A_v\,\partial_y f + A_w\,\partial_y g ,
\end{equation}
after which everything of Sections~\ref{sec:epsform}--\ref{sec:finding}
applies verbatim in $(x,y)$, with flatness re-certified in the chart.

\subsection{Two roots: joint charts}

A family whose alphabet involves \emph{two} independent roots needs a
chart rationalizing both simultaneously.  The construction is
iterative: rationalize the first root by its catalog chart; the second
radicand pulls back to a new polynomial $q_2(x,y)$, which defines a
second conic \emph{in the chart variables}; find a rational point on
it and parametrize by lines again.  The result is the joint charts
\textsf{Kallen12}, \textsf{Kallen13}, \textsf{Kallen23} used by the
thirteen two-root families.  Concretely \textsf{Kallen12} keeps $y$
and replaces $x$ by a rational function $x(y,s)$ of a line parameter
$s$, chosen so both $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ pull back to squares.
This is why the certified two-root records are written in variables
like $(y,s)$: they live in a joint chart.

\subsection{Three roots: the Galois algebra}
\label{sec:galois}

For the three-root families no joint chart exists --- provably.  The
three radicands define a triple cover whose simultaneous
rationalization would require the associated surface (the fibre
product of the three conic double covers) to be rational; for the root
triples of \texttt{CF300}-type families that surface is a K3 surface,
which admits no rational parametrization.  The obstruction is
geometric, not a failure of search.

The replacement is to stop parametrizing and work in the function
\emph{field extension} directly.  Adjoin the roots as symbols:
\begin{equation}
  E \;=\; \Q(v,w,\eps)\,[r_1,r_2,r_3]\ \big/\ \big(r_i^2 - q_i\big),
\end{equation}
an eight-dimensional algebra with basis $\{r_S = \prod_{i\in S} r_i\}$
over the subsets $S \subseteq \{1,2,3\}$.  Its \emph{Galois group} is
$(\Z/2)^3$, acting by the sign flips $r_i \to -r_i$; every function of
the roots splits into eight \emph{grades} (the characters of the
group), multiplication is grade-wise
($r_S\, r_T = \prod_{i\in S\cap T} q_i\; r_{S\triangle T}$), and
differentiation obeys
$\partial r_S = \tfrac12 r_S\, \sum_{i\in S}\partial\log q_i$.  This
is ``the Galois stuff'': not a technique bolted on, but the statement
that the solution space organizes into representations of the root
sign group, and all linear algebra (the strip equation, the modular
sampling, the reconstruction) can be carried out on the eight-component
coefficient vectors over the \emph{rational} base field --- with the
eight sign branches recombined at the end as a consistency
certificate.  Modular evaluation picks primes and points where each
$q_i$ is a nonzero quadratic residue, so all eight embeddings exist
over $\Fp$ and the grade decomposition is an honest discrete Fourier
transform over $(\Z/2)^3$.  The three-root families are the current
frontier: the machinery (algebra, strip assembly, modular solving,
branch certificates) is built and certified on synthetic cases; the
physical blocks of \texttt{CF300}'s deepest sector are where it is
being brought to bear.

\begin{remark}[Roots, charts, and the involution]
The class equivalence of Definition~\ref{def:class} interacts with
charts: the swap $v \leftrightarrow w$ maps $\lambda_2
\leftrightarrow \lambda_3$ and $4v{+}w^2 \leftrightarrow v^2{+}4w$, so
one chart per swap-pair suffices, and inside the K\"all\'en chart the
swap is the simple involution $(x,y)\to(1-x,1-y)$.
\end{remark}

\section{Integrating the system: transport and polylogarithms}
\label{sec:transport}

\subsection{Words along paths}

With the $\eps$-form certified, Eq.~\eqref{eq:pexp} reduces solving to
evaluating iterated integrals of dlog forms along paths from a base
point.  Two standard facts organize this:

\begin{definition}[Generalized polylogarithm]
For letters that are \emph{linear} in the integration parameter $t$
(i.e.\ $\varphi = t - a_i$ along the path), the iterated integrals are
\emph{generalized polylogarithms} (GPLs)
\begin{equation}
  G(a_1,\dots,a_k;\,t) = \int_0^{t}\frac{\dd t_1}{t_1 - a_1}\,
  G(a_2,\dots,a_k;\,t_1),
  \qquad G(;t)=1,
\end{equation}
with weight $k$; $G(\vec 0_k;t) = \tfrac{1}{k!}\log^k t$, and
$G(0,1;t) = -\mathrm{Li}_2(t)$ recovers Example~\ref{ex:li2}.
\end{definition}

Solving a family in practice (\emph{transport}) means: choose a base
point $p_0$ in the physical region \eqref{eq:region}; choose
axis-aligned or otherwise convenient path segments to the point of
interest; restrict the alphabet to each segment (each letter becomes a
polynomial in the path parameter --- segments are chosen so it is
\emph{linear}, else the segment is subdivided or re-parametrized);
and accumulate the words $R_{a_k}\cdots R_{a_1} G(\dots)$ order by
order to the required $\eps$-depth.  Path independence within the
region is guaranteed by flatness; crossing a letter's zero locus is
forbidden (that is analytic continuation, handled deliberately, not by
accident).

Two project-specific disciplines are worth knowing:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Depth budget.}  The $\eps$-depth to which a given master
  must be expanded is not uniform: a master multiplying a $1/\eps^2$
  coefficient in the assembled cross section needs two more orders
  than one multiplying a finite coefficient.  The required depth per
  master is computed \emph{before} transport by a reachability
  analysis through the assembly (of the double-real inventory, $203$
  masters need only the finite part, $131$ one pole deeper, $9$ deeper
  still), and transport carries exactly that depth --- weight-$k$
  words are expensive, and the budget is the difference between hours
  and days.
\item \textbf{Chart families transport in their chart.}  A family
  whose $\eps$-form lives in a chart is transported in the chart
  variables along paths chosen so all pulled-back letters stay linear
  in the path parameter; the result is pulled back to $(v,w)$ only at
  the evaluation points.
\end{itemize}

\section{Boundary conditions}
\label{sec:boundary}

\subsection{The counting problem}

The general solution \eqref{eq:pexp} leaves one constant vector
$\vec F(p_0) = \sum_k \eps^k \vec C^{(k)}$ per family --- naively
$347$ unknown constants per $\eps$-order for the whole inventory.  The
first principle of the boundary program is that almost all of them are
\emph{not new numbers}: they are determined by consistency, by
physics, or by constants already fixed lower in the lattice.  Only
what survives all constraints requires computing an integral, and each
such number is a \emph{boundary period}: the value of a cut phase-space
integral in an ordered limit --- a $\Q$-linear combination of
transcendental constants ($\zeta_2$, $\zeta_3$, $\log 2\cdot\zeta_2$,
\dots) at the relevant weight.

The constraints, in the order they are applied:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Normalization masters (volume rows).}  The simplest
  master of the simplest family is the pure phase-space volume, known
  in closed form (next example); its value seeds the lattice, and the
  registry maps it into every family where that sector reappears.
\item \textbf{Inherited rows.}  Families are solved in dependency
  order.  A subsector master solved in family $F_1$ \emph{is} (by the
  momentum-shift identification) a master of family $F_2$'s
  subsector; its already-known expansion becomes linear constraints
  on $F_2$'s boundary vector.
\item \textbf{Forbidden-mode (regularity) rows.}  Near a singular
  locus the local solutions behave as powers $u^{n + a\eps}$
  (Section~\ref{sec:endpoint}).  The integral \emph{representation}
  dictates which exponents are allowed: a cut phase-space integral has
  a definite power counting at $w\to0$, at $v\to0$, and on the soft
  surface $1-v-w\to0$, and any solution mode with an exponent outside
  the allowed set must have coefficient zero.  Each forbidden mode is
  one homogeneous linear condition on the boundary vector.  This is
  the workhorse: most constants die here.
\item \textbf{Regularity at spurious points.}  Zero loci of letters
  that lie \emph{inside} the physical region of a given integral (or
  of a subintegral) are spurious singularities for it; finiteness
  there kills the corresponding local modes.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Nullity of a block]
Assemble all constraints on a block's boundary vector into a matrix
$C$ (rows: constraints; columns: local boundary modes).  The number of
genuinely new periods of the block is $N_{\mathrm{new}} = \dim\ker C$,
computable \emph{before} evaluating anything.  Blocks are processed in
lattice order and periods deduplicated across blocks by sector
identity; for the double-real inventory this leaves a period budget of
a few dozen candidate periods, of which the confirmed genuinely new
tier is smaller still --- each then established by a dedicated exact
computation (Mellin--Barnes, regions, hypergeometric expansion), with
an independent numeric check that guides but never proves.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[The seed: massless phase-space volume]
\label{ex:volume}
The $n$-particle massless phase-space volume in $d$ dimensions,
$\Phi_n(q^2)$, is fixed by Lorentz invariance and scaling up to the
overall constant, which a short recursive computation gives as
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_n(q^2) \;=\; (q^2)^{\,(n-1)(d-2)/2\, -\, \text{(norm.)}}\;
  \frac{\big(2\pi\big)^{\,\ldots}\;
  \Gamma(d/2-1)^{\,n-1}\,\Gamma(d-2)^{\,\ldots}}
  {\Gamma\big((n-1)(d-2)/2\big)\,\Gamma\big(n(d-2)/2\big)}\cdot
  (\text{scheme factors}),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ a pure ratio of $\Gamma$-functions of $\eps$ --- for the
present purpose the only facts that matter are: (i) it is the
$a_i{=}1$, numerator-free master of the lowest sector; (ii) it is
computable in closed form directly from the definition, with no
differential equation; (iii) expanded in $\eps$ it delivers exact
$\zeta$-values that propagate upward through the inherited rows.  In
the same spirit, the handful of genuinely new periods higher up are
cut integrals evaluated at an ordered limit (e.g.\ the corner
$v,w\to0$ taken in a definite order), where they reduce to
$\Gamma$-function integrals or one-fold Mellin--Barnes
representations.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Boundary counting on Example~\ref{ex:li2}]
The $2\times2$ system had a two-component boundary vector per
$\eps$-order.  Constraints: (i) at $w\to0$ the local exponents are
$\{0,\,-\eps\}$ (eigenvalues of $\eps R_{w}$ with
$R_w=\mathrm{diag}(0,-1)$); if the physical integral behind $F_2$ is
known to be \emph{finite and vanishing} at $w=0$, the $w^{-\eps}$ mode
is forbidden and one constant dies.  (ii) $F_1$ is the volume-type
subsector master, inherited.  Nullity: $0$ --- no new period, and
indeed the solution $\mathrm{Li}_2(w)$ carried no free constant once
$F_2(0)=0$ was imposed.  This miniature is faithfully the shape of the
real bookkeeping.
\end{example}

\subsection{Where forbidden modes come from: a power-counting
derivation}
\label{sec:powercounting}

The forbidden-mode rows deserve a derivation, because they carry most
of the weight.  Consider a master defined as a cut phase-space
integral and examine the collinear limit $w\to0$.  In the phase-space
parametrization the limit corresponds to a corner where the measure
and the propagators scale with definite powers of $w$: schematically,
if the measure contributes $w^{\,\alpha-\beta\eps}\,\dd w$ and the
integrand's propagators contribute a bounded set of extra integer
powers, then the integral near the boundary is of the form
\begin{equation}
  I(w) \;=\; \int^{w}\!\dd w'\; (w')^{\,\alpha-\beta\eps}\,
  \rho(w')
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  I(w) \sim \sum_{n\ge n_0} c_n\, w^{\,n+1+\alpha-\beta\eps}
  \;+\; (\text{analytic}),
\end{equation}
with $\alpha,\beta$ fixed integers determined by the dimension $d=4-2\eps$
and the kinematics of the collinear configuration --- \emph{not} by
the details of the sector.  The representation therefore admits local
exponents only from the arithmetic progression
$\{\,n + a\eps:\ a \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{phys}}\,\}$ with a short,
computable list $\mathcal{A}_{\text{phys}}$ of allowed
$\eps$-slopes.  On the other side, the differential equation offers
the modes \eqref{eq:frobenius} with slopes from the eigenvalues of the
residue matrix.  Whenever the equation offers a slope the
representation forbids, the coefficient of that entire mode must
vanish: one exact homogeneous condition per forbidden slope per
master.  The same argument runs on the second collinear edge $v \to 0$
and on the soft surface $1-v-w\to0$ (where the scaling variable is the
distance to the surface).  Two practical warnings are baked into the
production rules: the allowed list is computed \emph{per chart} ---
transplanting the $(v,w)$ soft table into a chart frame is wrong
because the chart Jacobian shifts the measure powers; and inequivalent
Jordan blocks can hide a forbidden slope inside a log-degenerate mode,
which is why the substrate records eigenvalues \emph{and} Jordan data
per stratum.

\subsection{How many independent boundaries are there?}

The question ``how many independent ones?'' thus has a layered answer:
$347$ masters $\times$ depth constants \emph{a priori}; after volume
seeding, inheritance, forbidden modes, and regularity, the kernel
dimensions sum to a few dozen \emph{candidate} periods inventory-wide;
after cross-block deduplication the genuinely new tier is the short
list actually computed.  The discipline that keeps this honest: the
nullity is computed and recorded \emph{before} any evaluation, so
``how many numbers must we produce'' is a theorem about the constraint
matrix, not a hope.

\section{Singular structure: reading the matrix at the endpoints}
\label{sec:endpoint}

\subsection{Why the singularity is ``in the matrix''}

The connection's poles are the letters' zero loci.  Near a locus, say
$w \to 0$ with letter $w$, the $\eps$-form reads
\begin{equation}
  \partial_w \vec F = \frac{\eps\,R_{w}}{w}\,\vec F + (\text{regular}),
\end{equation}
whose local (Frobenius) solutions are
\begin{equation}
  \vec F \;\sim\; w^{\,\eps R_w}\,\vec h(w)
  \;=\; \sum_{\text{modes}} w^{\,n + a\eps}\,(\log w)^{j}\,
  \big(\text{const} + O(w)\big),
  \label{eq:frobenius}
\end{equation}
with the exponents $a$ the eigenvalues of $R_w$, integer offsets $n$
from the expansion of the regular part, and logs from Jordan blocks.
\emph{This is the answer to ``why is the singularity in the matrix'':}
the residue matrix at a letter is precisely the complete catalogue of
the solutions' local behaviour there --- exponents from its
eigenvalues, log powers from its Jordan structure.  Nothing about the
endpoint behaviour needs to be discovered from the solution; it is read
off the connection, exactly, before any integration.

\subsection{Recovering the singular expansion, and why it must not be
expanded away}

Two complementary uses:

\paragraph{(i) Matching.}  The global solution \eqref{eq:pexp} is a
combination of GPLs; near $w=0$ each GPL has a known expansion in
$w^{a\eps}\,(\log w)^j$ obtained by matching against
\eqref{eq:frobenius}.  This matching is how boundary values ``at'' a
singular point are defined (the constant term in the mode of exponent
$0$), and how the forbidden-mode rows of Section~\ref{sec:boundary}
are imposed.

\paragraph{(ii) Endpoint distributions.}  The cross section requires
integrating the masters against smooth test functions up to the
endpoint $w \to 1$ (and its analogues).  A mode
$(1-w)^{-1 + a\eps}$ integrates to
\begin{equation}
  (1-w)^{-1+a\eps} \;=\;
  \frac{1}{a\eps}\,\delta(1-w)
  \;+\;
  \sum_{k\ge0} \frac{(a\eps)^k}{k!}
  \left[\frac{\log^k(1-w)}{1-w}\right]_+ ,
  \label{eq:plus}
\end{equation}
the standard plus-distribution expansion (derive it by integrating
both sides against a test function $f(w)$ and splitting off $f(1)$).
The crucial observation: if one first expands the \emph{master} in
$\eps$, the left side becomes the log tower
$\tfrac{1}{1-w}\sum_k \frac{(a\eps)^k}{k!}\log^k(1-w)$, whose
term-by-term integrals are divergent --- \emph{the $\delta$-function
content is gone}.  The distributional identity \eqref{eq:plus} only
exists for the \emph{unexpanded} power.  Hence the pipeline's rule:
masters are delivered with their exact indicial data (which modes
$n + a\eps$, with which constant prefactors) alongside the expanded
GPL representation; the endpoint extraction consumes the mode
decomposition, and only afterwards is everything expanded in $\eps$.
``Recovering the singularity'' is thus a resummation-free bookkeeping
step: the matrix gave the exponents, the boundary program gave the
mode coefficients, and \eqref{eq:plus} turns them into distributions.

\section{Certification: the epistemology of the pipeline}
\label{sec:certification}

Every stage above pairs a \emph{search}, which may be heuristic,
numerical, modular, or downright opportunistic, with a
\emph{certificate}, which is exact and independent of how the
candidate was found:
\begin{itemize}
\item a derived family system is accepted with an exact flatness
  identity;
\item a class $\eps$-form is accepted only by exact reconstruction of
  the stored form from constant residues (never by the shape of some
  intermediate output);
\item a completed family is accepted by the exact gauge identity
  $T^{-1}AT - T^{-1}\dd T = \eps \sum_a R_a \dd\log\varphi_a$ together
  with flatness of the result and exact constancy of the residues ---
  checked either symbolically or by a modular certificate with
  reconstruction at held-out primes;
\item a boundary period enters the ledger only with an exact proof
  chain (its defining integral, variable maps, mode, exact value)
  \emph{plus} an independent numeric check --- and the numeric check
  guides but never proves;
\item transported values are cross-checked against independent
  lower-depth recomputations and high-precision numerics at interior
  points.
\end{itemize}
The reason for the discipline is practical: with $O(10^3)$ blocks and
$O(10^2)$ families, any silent wrong answer would be fatally
expensive to locate later.  The certificates make the final object ---
$347$ masters as certified $\eps$-forms plus boundary data --- a
self-verifying artifact.

\paragraph{Glossary.}
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[Master integral] Basis element of the $\Q(v,w,\eps)$-vector
space of a family's integrals modulo IBP.
\item[Family / canonical family] Propagator set closed under scalar
products; canonical = representative modulo momentum shifts
($GL(2,\Z)$, cut-preserving), named \texttt{CF}$n$.
\item[Sector] Subset of propagators actually present (positive
powers); subsectors arise by deleting lines.
\item[Block] Strongly connected group of masters in one family's DE;
a diagonal block of the triangular connection.
\item[Class] Equivalence class of blocks under basis permutation
optionally composed with $v \leftrightarrow w$; 173 in this project.
\item[Connection] The matrix one-form $A$ in $\dd\vec I = A\vec I$.
\item[Flatness] $\dd A = A\wedge A$; integrability of the pair
$(A_v,A_w)$.
\item[$\eps$-form / canonical form] $A = \eps\sum_a R_a
\dd\log\varphi_a$ with constant $R_a$.
\item[Letter / alphabet] The functions $\varphi_a$ / their set.
\item[Residue matrix] The constant $R_a$; eigenvalues give local
exponents at $\varphi_a=0$.
\item[Gauge transformation] Basis change $\vec I = T \vec F$ acting on
$A$ by $T^{-1}AT - T^{-1}\dd T$.
\item[Strip] One off-diagonal block row being completed; its unknown
gauge $D$ obeys the linear equation \eqref{eq:strip}.
\item[Root] Independent square class $\sqrt{q(v,w)}$ occurring in an
alphabet.
\item[Chart] Rational substitution making a root (or root set)
rational; \textsf{Kallen1/2/3}, \textsf{Q4a/b},
\textsf{Bilinear115}, joint \textsf{Kallen12/13/23}.
\item[Galois / multiquadratic algebra] $\Q(v,w,\eps)[r_1..r_k]/(r_i^2
- q_i)$ with sign group $(\Z/2)^k$; the three-root technology.
\item[Transport] Building the path-ordered solution as GPL words to a
budgeted $\eps$-depth.
\item[Boundary period] Genuinely new constant surviving all boundary
constraints; a cut phase-space integral at an ordered limit.
\item[Nullity] $\dim\ker C$ of a block's boundary-constraint matrix =
number of new periods, computed before evaluation.
\item[Indicial data / modes] The exponents $n + a\eps$ and log powers
at a singular locus, read from the residue matrices.
\end{description}

\subsection{One family end to end}

To fix the whole chain in the mind, here is the biography of the real
family \texttt{CF12} ($14$ masters, root-free), compressed to its
decision points:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Reduction} named it \texttt{CF12} (a canonical family
  after momentum-shift merging) and produced its $14$ masters.
\item \textbf{DE derivation} built $(A_v,A_w)$ by differentiating each
  master and IBP-reducing; the exact flatness identity certified the
  pair.  Ordered by sectors the connection is block-triangular with
  $9$ diagonal blocks.
\item \textbf{Classification} matched each block, by exact
  permutation-plus-swap identity, to one of the $173$ class
  representatives; \texttt{CF12} introduced no new class.
\item \textbf{Assembly + completion}: the stored class $\eps$-forms
  were composed in (as $T_{\rm blockdiag}$); the finite-field strip
  solver completed the off-diagonal blocks row by row (many with the
  trivial $D=0$: their couplings were already constant-residue
  dlogs); a final constant $T(\eps)$ concentrated the
  regulator; the family certificate re-derived the whole gauge
  identity exactly.  Resulting alphabet:
  $\{v,\,w,\,1{-}v,\,1{-}w,\,v{+}w,\,1{-}v{-}w\}$.
\item \textbf{Transport} assembled the masters as GPL words in those
  six letters to each master's budgeted depth.
\item \textbf{Boundary}: volume seeding through its lowest sector,
  inheritance from subsector families, and forbidden modes at
  $v\to0$, $w\to0$, $1{-}v{-}w\to0$ left nullity zero --- no new
  period; the family is fully determined.
\item \textbf{Endpoint}: its indicial data at $w\to1$-type loci ride
  along with the GPL representation for the later distribution
  extraction.
\end{enumerate}
A two-root family (say \texttt{CF231}) tells the same story with two
extra chapters: its connection is pulled into the joint chart
\textsf{Kallen23} before step 4, and its completion meets genuinely
hard strips whose ansatz spans thousands of unknowns --- which is
exactly where the modular linear algebra earns its keep.  A three-root
family replaces the chart chapter with the Galois algebra of
Section~\ref{sec:galois}, and its story is still being written.
