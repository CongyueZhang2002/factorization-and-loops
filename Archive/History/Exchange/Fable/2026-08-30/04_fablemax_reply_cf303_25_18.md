## B. Resonance and the valuation bounds (escape i)

**B2 (the place resonance actually hides — do not skip).** Deligne's comparison needs normal crossings. Your 16 curves (degrees up to 3) plus infinity will have non-transverse intersections and tangencies; blow up the finitely many bad points, and on each exceptional component the residue is the *sum* of the residues of the branches through the point (plus multiplicity corrections for tangency towers). A sum of nilpotents need not be nilpotent: exceptional components are exactly where nonzero — possibly positive-integer — eigenvalues can appear, and the corresponding modes are point-supported gauge denominators with higher *joint* vanishing that a per-component census undercounts. So the complete, finite, decidable answer to your question B is: (i) compute D's singular/tangency points once; (ii) blow up to normal crossings; (iii) list residue spectra on all components including exceptionals and infinity; (iv) admissible extra valuations = positive-integer eigenvalues with Levelt multiplicities, local Fuchs/residue-theorem relations pinning the infinity allowance. Expected outcome: empty or a handful of point-supported modes — either way, escape (i) is then settled by one bounded computation, as you hoped.

**B3 (what escape (i) cannot cover — flag).** No valuation analysis on D bounds apparent singularities, because their defining property is a divisor component absent from D. Treat them as a separate, degree-graded candidate family for the oracle (C2), not as part of the census.
